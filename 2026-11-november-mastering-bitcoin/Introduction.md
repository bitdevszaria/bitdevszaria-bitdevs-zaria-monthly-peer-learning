# November Peer Learning & Reading Sessions

**Theme:** *Mastering Bitcoin*  
**Authors:** Andreas M. Antonopoulos and David A. Harding (with contributors in later editions)  
**Community:** BitDevs Zaria  
**Required reading:** The introductory foundations and topics covering how Bitcoin works, keys and addresses, wallets, transactions, the peer-to-peer network, and the blockchain. Use the edition available to your team; chapter numbers vary by edition, so follow topic headings.

## Purpose

Build a practical mental model of Bitcoin’s transactions, keys and wallets, peer-to-peer network, and blockchain through selected foundational topics in *Mastering Bitcoin*.

## Learning objectives

By the end of the month, participants should be able to:

- Describe how a Bitcoin payment is created, propagated, validated, and recorded.
- Distinguish private keys, public keys, addresses, wallets, transactions, and the blockchain.
- Discuss how Bitcoin nodes communicate and independently verify transactions and blocks.
- Identify security practices and misconceptions relevant to users and developers.

## Session format

Members are organized into teams, each with a chaperone. Teams meet biweekly—at least twice during the month—to read, learn, and discuss. The chaperone coordinates meetings, guides discussion, and makes sure every member has an opportunity to contribute. Each team chaperone shares dates, times, and venue or online link with their team.

| Session | Suggested focus | Team activity |
|---|---|---|
| Session 1 — Foundations and transactions | Introductory and transaction-related topics: how Bitcoin works, keys and addresses, wallets, and the structure and flow of transactions. Check the corresponding chapters in your edition. | Compare concepts and note questions or security implications. |

Teams may adjust pacing while completing the month’s core reading. Bring review notes, diagrams, a simple sketch of payment flow, or a glossary of key concepts to help the discussion.

## Discussion questions

1. In the book’s explanation, what are the main steps from creating a transaction to its confirmation?
2. What is the difference between a private key, public key, address, and wallet?
3. Why is a wallet better understood as a tool for managing keys than as a container holding coins?
4. How can a Bitcoin node check a transaction or block without trusting the person who sent it?
5. What information is shared across the peer-to-peer network, and what can network participants infer?
6. What does the blockchain record, and how do transactions refer to earlier outputs?
7. Which user security practices follow from the way keys and wallets work?
8. Which book explanation, diagram, or example helped most, and what remains unclear?

## Before each meeting

- Confirm which edition of *Mastering Bitcoin* your team is using; chapter numbers may differ.
- Read the selected topics and note page or chapter references in your own copy.
- Bring one question and one concept you can explain to a beginner.
- Chaperones share the meeting time and venue or online link, invite members to prepare, and make space for all voices.

## End-of-month output

Each team produces a brief concept map or verbal walkthrough connecting keys and wallets, transaction creation, peer-to-peer relay, node validation, and blockchain confirmation. Record remaining questions and choose follow-up reading as needed.

Suggested team record: meeting dates, chaperone, members present, key takeaways, questions to revisit, and the next reading commitment.
