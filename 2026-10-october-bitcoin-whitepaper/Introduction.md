# October Peer Learning & Reading Sessions

**Theme:** Bitcoin Whitepaper  
**Community:** BitDevs Zaria  
**Required reading:** Satoshi Nakamoto, [“Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System”](https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf) — read the English original.

## Purpose

Work through the Bitcoin whitepaper together and build a shared understanding of the problem it addresses and the design it proposes.

## Learning objectives

By the end of the month, participants should be able to:

- Explain the problem the paper seeks to solve and the role of a peer-to-peer network.
- Describe at a high level how transactions, proof-of-work, block linkage, and network consensus fit together.
- Discuss the assumptions, trade-offs, and open questions raised by the paper.
- Explain the paper’s central ideas clearly to another community member.

## Session format

Members are organized into teams, each with a chaperone. Teams meet biweekly—at least twice during the month—to read, learn, and discuss. The chaperone coordinates meetings, guides discussion, and makes sure every member has an opportunity to contribute. Each team chaperone shares dates, times, and venue or online link with their team.

| Session | Suggested focus | Team activity |
|---|---|---|
| Session 1 — First reading and core proposal | Problem statement, transaction model, timestamping, proof-of-work, and network sections. | Discuss the reading, identify unfamiliar terms, and record questions. |
| Session 2 — Design, incentives, and reflection | Network operation, incentives, privacy considerations, and the paper’s conclusion. | Revisit Session 1 questions and share the team’s key takeaways. |

Teams may adjust pacing while completing the month’s core reading. Read the complete whitepaper (nine sections). A glossary or Bitcoin fundamentals notes may be used to clarify terminology, but prioritize understanding the original paper. Follow the section topics in your edition if titles or numbering differ.

## Discussion questions

1. What limitation of earlier digital-cash systems does the whitepaper aim to address?
2. What does the paper mean by a peer-to-peer electronic cash system, and how does a payment move through it?
3. Why is proof-of-work used, and how does it make changing the transaction history difficult?
4. How do blocks, timestamps, and the chain with the most proof-of-work contribute to agreement across the network?
5. What role do incentives play in encouraging nodes to support the network?
6. What does the paper say about privacy, and what does it not promise?
7. Which parts of the design are easiest to misunderstand? Explain them in your own words.
8. What questions remain after reading, and what additional resource could help answer them?

## Before each meeting

- Read the whitepaper once before the first team meeting and note unfamiliar terms and questions.
- Bring one passage or idea you found important and be ready to explain why.
- Chaperones share the meeting time and venue or online link, invite members to prepare, and make space for all voices.

## End-of-month output

Each team shares a short summary of the whitepaper’s main proposal, one important design insight, and one question for continued learning. Teams may also agree on a volunteer to introduce the next month’s book.

Suggested team record: meeting dates, chaperone, members present, key takeaways, questions to revisit, and the next reading commitment.
